# Methods generator — black-box user audit

**Scope:** the rendered Methods generator page, exercised as a microscopy user
would exercise it.
**Method:** 116 simulated sessions driven through Chromium/Playwright against the
`mkdocs build` output. Every session selects an instrument in the dropdown,
picks the imaging method, confirms the physical light path, ticks hardware
checkboxes, presses **Add to methods**, and reads the resulting text. No DTO was
hand-built, no generator function was called directly, and no synthetic
instrument fixture was used — all 22 active records plus the retired Leica TCS
SP5 were driven through their real controls.
**Verbatim output for all 116 scenarios:** [`methods_generator_ui_audit_transcripts.md`](methods_generator_ui_audit_transcripts.md).

Nothing in the generator was changed while auditing it.

---

## A. Executive assessment

The generator is impressively close to useful. For a *single* acquisition, built
in one pass, on an instrument with a complete record, it produces prose a
microscopist could genuinely paste into a manuscript after filling in
experiment-specific settings: the method is named correctly and technique-first
("Stimulated emission depletion (STED) imaging was performed using…", "Image
scanning microscopy (ISM; Airyscan) was performed using…"), the objective is
rendered with magnification, NA, immersion, vendor name and catalogue number,
the light path reads as a path rather than a list, and the reporting
recommendations are technique-aware (STED depletion settings, TIRF incidence
angle, SIM reconstruction, SMLM frame counts and drift correction, spinning-disk
exposure and disk settings). The separation of finished prose from a **Review
before publication** block is the right architecture and mostly works.

It is not ready for unsupervised real users. The central failure is not prose
quality; it is that **the control panel and the generated entry disagree about
what "this acquisition" means**, and that disagreement produces text asserting
hardware the user did not select for the acquisition being described.

**Scientific reliability — not yet.** Changing the imaging method clears
route-scoped selections (lasers, filters, detectors) but leaves
instrument-scoped ones (objectives, modules, scanners, modulators, confirmed
actions) ticked. Every subsequent entry therefore inherits them. A user who
images Airyscan and then DIC on the LSM 880 gets a DIC paragraph stating "The
AiryScan Detector/Module was used"; STED followed by a confocal reference puts
the Easy3D STED module in the confocal paragraph; SIM followed by widefield puts
the 3D-SIM module in the widefield paragraph. These are false statements about
the experiment, generated by a workflow the page itself recommends.
Independently, two physically different spinning-disk systems render under one
identical name because the identity sentence uses manufacturer + model rather
than the record's `display_name`.

**Quality of generated prose — good, with systemic tics.** Sentences are
combined sensibly (microscope + objective + modules, then illumination + optics
+ detection). The recurring problems are structural, not typographical: readout
selections are appended as one-clause stubs ("FLIM data were acquired."), every
module and every confirmed action gets its own sentence so over-ticking collapses
into a wall of parallel clauses, light-source labels are used without articles
("Excitation was provided by 640 nm laser"), and internal labels leak —
`position EMP_BF`, `Power HyD S SP pos 1`, `the ONI Nanoimager other microscope`,
`Unknown Camera`.

**Usability of the selection workflow — the weakest area.** Selections are wiped
when the method changes even if the physical path does not change (Zeiss TIRF
widefield → TIRF is the same recorded route; all three hardware choices vanish
silently). Correcting a forgotten checkbox after generating adds a *second*
acquisition rather than updating the first. **Clear all** empties the draft but
leaves every checkbox ticked. None of these is announced to the user.

**Behaviour under incomplete metadata — safe in the designed cases, unsafe in
the undesigned ones.** Where the record marks a route's topology as incomplete
the generator says so, which is exactly right. But there is no check at all for
"this entry has no detector", "no light source" or "no objective": the Airyscan,
TIRF and SIM entries above are emitted with no detection sentence and nothing in
the review block. Separately, placeholder identities escape into prose ("Images
were recorded using Unknown Camera.", "Images were recorded using Zeiss.") with
no request to confirm them, and instrument-level metadata gaps are reported in
acquisitions that never touched the affected component.

**Multi-acquisition sections — currently the most dangerous mode.** Ten of the
fourteen multi-acquisition sections contained at least one false or materially
incomplete entry. The microscope identity and software sentences also repeat
verbatim in every entry, which reads as concatenated form output rather than a
Methods section.

**Verdict:** safe today as a *staff-assisted* drafting aid for one acquisition at
a time. Not safe as a self-service tool, and specifically not safe for the
multi-acquisition workflow the page advertises, until the selection-scope
defects (D-01 … D-04) are fixed.

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Scientific reliability | **Fails** — 4 Critical defects produce prose asserting unselected hardware |
| Prose quality | **Acceptable** — good sentence construction, systemic stub/label/article problems |
| Selection workflow | **Fails** — silent clearing, silent retention, duplicate entries, non-clearing "Clear all" |
| Incomplete metadata | **Partial** — good route-level fail-safes, no entry-level completeness check, placeholders leak |
| Multi-acquisition | **Fails** — 10/14 sections contained a false or materially incomplete entry |

### One structural observation about the method → path → hardware workflow

In the present catalogue **every imaging method maps to exactly one recorded
physical path**, and the generator auto-selects it. The "confirm the physical
path" step is therefore never a real choice today, and the multi-path branches of
the workflow could not be exercised against real records. What *is* exercised —
and what fails — is the inverse case: several methods sharing one path (Zeiss
TIRF widefield/TIRF, Abberior confocal/STED/RESOLFT, OMX widefield/TIRF/SIM/SMLM,
BC43 confocal/widefield/brightfield, transmitted brightfield/phase/DIC
everywhere). That is where the state handling breaks (D-02).

Checks that **passed**: a method never exposes a path the record does not
associate with it; a route is never silently reinterpreted as a different
imaging method (the method label always drives the opening sentence, so a
point-scanning path used for STED says STED, and used for ISM says ISM — see
A01, A53); one acquisition never accumulates two paths without a
`[PLEASE VERIFY]`; switching instrument resets everything (B16); filters and
detectors offered are the ones the selected path records; and a filter reachable
from two routes stays selectable from both (B10, A14).

## C. Prioritised defect list

### CRITICAL

#### D-01 — Instrument-scoped selections are never cleared, so hardware from one acquisition is asserted in the next

**Severity:** Critical
**Affects:** every instrument that offers modules, scanners, magnification
changers, optical modulators or illumination logic; most visibly Zeiss LSM 880,
Abberior STED, Deltavision OMX, Nikon Crest V3, STELLARIS.

**Reproduction (C08):**
1. Microscope → *Zeiss LSM 880 with AiryScan*.
2. Imaging method → *ISM (AiryScan)*.
3. Tick the ZEN acquisition-software statement, the 63×/1.4 Oil objective, the
   488 nm laser, and **AiryScan Detector/Module**.
4. Reference → `Airyscan`; **Add to methods**.
5. Imaging method → *Confocal point scanning*. Tick *PMT (Ch1)*. Reference →
   `confocal`; **Add to methods**.
6. Imaging method → *DIC*. Tick the halogen lamp and *Transmitted light PMT*.
   Reference → `DIC`; **Add to methods**.

**Actual text (entries 2 and 3, verbatim):**

```text
confocal

Point-scanning confocal imaging was performed using the Zeiss LSM 880 with AiryScan inverted microscope. Imaging was performed with a 63x/1.4 Oil objective (Zeiss C-Plan-APOCHROMAT 63x/1.4 Oil DIC UV-VIS-IR, 421782-9900). The AiryScan Detector/Module was used (Zeiss Airyscan). Instrument control and image acquisition were performed using Zeiss ZEN 2.3 SP1 (black edition) (version FP3 HF 30 FP3 (build 14.0.30.201)).

Images were recorded using PMT (Ch1).

DIC

Differential interference contrast (DIC) imaging was performed using the Zeiss LSM 880 with AiryScan inverted microscope. Imaging was performed with a 63x/1.4 Oil objective (Zeiss C-Plan-APOCHROMAT 63x/1.4 Oil DIC UV-VIS-IR, 421782-9900). The AiryScan Detector/Module was used (Zeiss Airyscan). Instrument control and image acquisition were performed using Zeiss ZEN 2.3 SP1 (black edition) (version FP3 HF 30 FP3 (build 14.0.30.201)).

Transmitted-light illumination was provided by halogen lamp (Zeiss Transmitted light illumination (HAL 100 / LED)). Images were recorded using Transmitted light PMT.
```

A transmitted-light DIC image cannot be acquired with the Airyscan detector.
Same defect, other shapes:

* **C04** — confocal reference entry: `The Easy3D STED module was used (Abberior easy3D STED).`
* **C03** — widefield reference entry: `The 3D-SIM Module was used (GE Healthcare 3D-SIM Illumination Engine).`
* **D04** — RESOLFT entry: `The Easy3D STED module was used (Abberior easy3D STED).`
* **B22** — single session, confocal entry after switching away from ISM.
* **C01 / C02 / C06 / C10** — the objective variant: `Imaging was performed with a 20x/0.8 Air objective (…) and a 63x/1.4 Oil objective (…)` where only the 63× was ticked for that acquisition.

**Expected:** an entry must describe only what is ticked *for that entry*.
Changing the imaging method should clear, or at minimum visibly re-confirm,
every acquisition-scoped selection — not only the route-scoped ones.

**Likely root cause:** `assets/javascripts/methods_generator_app.js:1436-1439`.
The `method-list` change handler clears exactly
`["route", "readout", "light", "det", "filter", "filterposition", "splitter"]`.
`obj`, `module`, `scanner`, `magnification-changer`, `optical-modulator`,
`illumination-logic` and `confirmed` are not in the list, so they persist.

**Suggested fix:** decide the scope explicitly rather than by omission. Clear
every prefix that describes *this acquisition* (add `module`, `scanner`,
`magnification-changer`, `optical-modulator`, `illumination-logic`), and either
clear `obj` too or, if a retained objective is desirable, show it as a distinct
"carried over from the previous entry — confirm" state rather than silently
re-asserting it. `confirmed` actions (software, incubation, autofocus) are
genuinely session-level and can reasonably persist, but the generator should
then say so in the UI.

#### D-02 — Two different instruments are given the same name in prose

**Severity:** Critical
**Affects:** `3i CSU-W1 Spinning Disk` and `3i Marianas CSU-W1 Spinning Disk Med C`
(both records carry `manufacturer: 3i / Zeiss`, `model: Marianas CSU-W1 Spinning
Disk Confocal`, with distinct `display_name`s). Any future pair of records
sharing manufacturer + model is affected identically.

**Reproduction (C11):** build a spinning-disk entry on *3i CSU-W1 Spinning Disk*
(reference `system A`), add it; switch the microscope dropdown to *3i Marianas
CSU-W1 Spinning Disk Med C*, build an equivalent entry (reference `system B`),
add it.

**Actual text:**

```text
system A

Spinning-disk confocal imaging was performed using the 3i / Zeiss Marianas CSU-W1 Spinning Disk Confocal inverted microscope. …

system B

Spinning-disk confocal imaging was performed using the 3i / Zeiss Marianas CSU-W1 Spinning Disk Confocal inverted microscope. Imaging was performed with a 63x/1.4 Oil objective (Zeiss Plan-Apochromat 63x/1.4 NA Oil, 420780-9900-000). …
```

A reader cannot tell that two different microscopes were used, and "system B" is
described under a name that in the facility record belongs to the other
instrument.

**Expected:** `…using the 3i Marianas CSU-W1 Spinning Disk Med C (3i / Zeiss
Marianas CSU-W1 Spinning Disk Confocal), an inverted microscope.` — i.e. the
record's `display_name` first, the manufacturer/model as a parenthetical.

**Likely root cause:** `scripts/dashboard/instrument_view.py:944-955` builds
`instrument_reference` as `the {manufacturer} {model} {stand} microscope` and
only falls back to `display_name` when manufacturer and model are both absent.
The method-first opening sentence in the JS
(`methods_generator_app.js:1645-1650`) consumes that field. The repository
already contains a correct implementation — `_microscope_sentence()` in
`scripts/dashboard/methods_export.py:270-306` prefers `display_name`, adds
manufacturer/model parenthetically only when it is not already implied, and
suppresses placeholder stand values — but it is only reachable through the
`base_sentence` fallback path (which is why the retired SP5 in A34 renders
correctly and everything else does not).

**Suggested fix:** derive `instrument_reference` from the same logic as
`_microscope_sentence()`, or have the JS build the opening sentence from
`display_name` + the identity parenthetical rather than from
`instrument_reference`. One identity builder, not two.

#### D-03 — Route-scoped selections are wiped when the method changes even if the physical path does not

**Severity:** Critical (silent data loss producing materially incomplete prose)
**Affects:** every instrument where several methods share one recorded route —
Zeiss TIRF (widefield/TIRF), Abberior (confocal/STED/RESOLFT), Deltavision OMX
(widefield/TIRF/SIM/SMLM), Andor BC43 (confocal/widefield/brightfield), ONI
(TIRF/SMLM), LSM 880 (confocal/ISM), and every transmitted-light
brightfield/phase/DIC group.

**Reproduction (B09):**
1. Microscope → *Zeiss TIRF*; Imaging method → *Widefield fluorescence*.
2. Tick the 63×/1.46 Oil objective, the 561 nm laser, *Filter set 43 HE (DsRed)*,

## D. Prose-quality review

These are patterns across otherwise technically correct outputs, not individual
typos.

**1. Assembly is frame-driven, so anything without a frame becomes a stub.**
The generator has good frames — `{method} imaging was performed using {instrument}.`,
`Imaging was performed with a {objective}.`, `{role} was provided by {sources}.`,
`The light path included {positions} in the {holder}.`, `Images were recorded
using {detectors}.` — and it merges same-frame items well (four lasers become
one clause; two filter positions become one clause). Everything that has no
frame is appended as its own short sentence: readouts (`FLIM data were
acquired.`), modulators (`STED beam shaping used Abberior easy3D SLM.`),
illumination logic (`Adaptive illumination used RESCue STED.`). The fix is
structural: give these a slot in an existing sentence rather than a sentence of
their own. `Fluorescence lifetimes were recorded on the same confocal path using
the FALCON FLIM module.` does the work of two current sentences.

**2. One template per category, no grouping across categories.** Modules and
confirmed actions each render independently, so the paragraph length grows
linearly with ticks (D-12). Real Methods prose groups: *equipment the microscope
had*, *what was done with it*, *what the software was*. Three frames would
replace eleven sentences.

**3. Database labels are used as prose nouns.** `Power HyD S SP pos 1`,
`position EMP_BF`, `Unknown Camera`, `NIR dichroic position`, `other microscope`,
`Spectral Imaging` in title case, `MXR00547 V3 DualCam-GFP/mCherry 2 Bands
Celesta Set`. The repository clearly intends to prevent this — the
`_publication_inventory_label()` docstring says so explicitly — but the
`display_label` fallback and the separate `instrument_reference` builder route
around the intent. A single "publication label" function with a hard rule (no
slot numbers, no position ids, no vocabulary placeholders, no bare "Unknown")
applied to every category would close most of this.

**4. Grammatical agreement with generated labels.** Source labels are inserted
without articles because the label is sometimes a count noun ("640 nm laser")
and sometimes a mass phrase ("white light laser"). Deciding the article at label
construction time (`a 640 nm laser`, `the STELLARIS white light laser`) fixes it
once.

**5. Verb choice is driven by record completeness rather than by physics.**
"Excitation was provided by" vs "Illumination was provided by" differ only in
whether the YAML has a `role` key, so two identical confocal acquisitions on
different instruments read differently. Where the route is a fluorescence
excitation path, the role can be derived; the prompt should be reserved for
genuinely ambiguous sources.

**6. Repetition across entries.** Each entry re-states the microscope, the
objective and the software in full. For a two-entry section that is tolerable;
for the three-entry sections (C08, C10) it is not. A Methods section normally
introduces the microscope once and then says "on the same system" or "as above".
An entry-aware assembly step could suppress repeated identity clauses after the
first mention of an instrument.

**7. What is genuinely good and should not be lost.** The technique-first
opening; objective rendering with catalogue numbers; the holder+position
construction ("… in the CSU-W1 Emission Wheel"), which is exactly the right way
to distinguish a filter from its wheel when a position *is* selected;
`Samples were observed through eyepieces.` for visual-only observation; and the
clean separation of the review block from the prose.
